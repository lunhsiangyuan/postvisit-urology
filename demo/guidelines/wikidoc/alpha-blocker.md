# Alpha-1 Adrenergic Receptor Blockers

## Definition

Alpha-1 adrenergic receptor blockers (alpha-blockers) are a class of medications that selectively antagonize alpha-1 adrenergic receptors, relaxing smooth muscle in the prostate, bladder neck, and prostatic capsule. They are the most commonly prescribed first-line pharmacotherapy for moderate-to-severe lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) secondary to benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH).

## Mechanism of Action

Alpha-1 adrenergic receptors mediate smooth muscle contraction in the lower urinary tract. Three subtypes have been identified with distinct tissue distributions:

- **Alpha-1A**: Predominant subtype in prostatic stromal smooth muscle, bladder neck, and prostatic capsule. Mediates approximately 70% of prostatic smooth muscle contraction. Primary therapeutic target for LUTS/BPH.
- **Alpha-1B**: Predominant subtype in vascular smooth muscle. Blockade causes vasodilation and orthostatic hypotension. Not clinically useful for LUTS.
- **Alpha-1D**: Found in detrusor muscle, spinal cord, and sacral parasympathetic neurons. May contribute to storage symptoms (frequency, urgency). Also present in vascular smooth muscle.

By blocking alpha-1A receptors, these agents reduce the dynamic component of bladder outlet obstruction (smooth muscle tone), decreasing urethral resistance and improving urinary flow without affecting prostate volume.

## Pharmacology

### Uroselective Agents (Preferred)

**Tamsulosin (Flomax)**
- Dose: 0.4 mg once daily, 30 minutes after a meal
- Selectivity: Alpha-1A >> alpha-1D (super-selective)
- Minimal titration needed; can start at therapeutic dose
- Most widely prescribed alpha-blocker worldwide

**Silodosin (Rapaflo/Urief)**
- Dose: 8 mg once daily with a meal (4 mg if moderate renal impairment, CrCl 30-50 mL/min)
- Selectivity: Highest alpha-1A selectivity (162:1 ratio alpha-1A to alpha-1B)
- Lowest risk of cardiovascular side effects; highest rate of retrograde ejaculation (~28%)

**Alfuzosin (Uroxatral)**
- Dose: 10 mg extended-release once daily after a meal
- Selectivity: Functional uroselectivity (tissue-selective rather than receptor-selective)
- Lowest rate of ejaculatory dysfunction among alpha-blockers

### Non-Selective Agents

**Doxazosin (Cardura)**
- Dose: 1 mg initially, titrate to 4-8 mg daily
- Non-selective across all alpha-1 subtypes
- Requires dose titration due to first-dose hypotension
- Extended-release formulation (GITS) reduces cardiovascular side effects

**Terazosin (Hytrin)**
- Dose: 1 mg at bedtime initially, titrate to 5-10 mg daily
- Non-selective; requires slow dose titration
- Also indicated for hypertension (dual benefit in patients with concurrent HTN and BPH)

## Indications

- **Primary indication**: Moderate-to-severe LUTS/BPH (IPSS >=8)
- **Acute urinary retention**: Tamsulosin 0.4 mg for 2-3 days before trial without catheter (improves voiding trial success by approximately 30%)
- **Ureteral stone expulsion (medical expulsive therapy)**: Tamsulosin 0.4 mg for distal ureteral stones (relaxes ureteral smooth muscle); efficacy debated post-SUSPEND trial but still used selectively for stones 5-10 mm
- **Chronic prostatitis/CPPS**: Some evidence for symptom relief as adjunct therapy
- **Combination therapy**: With 5-alpha reductase inhibitors for large prostates (>40 mL) with moderate-severe LUTS; with beta-3 agonists or anticholinergics for mixed storage/voiding symptoms

## Clinical Efficacy

- **IPSS improvement**: Mean reduction of 4-6 points (30-45% improvement)
- **Qmax improvement**: Mean increase of 2-3 mL/s
- **Onset of action**: Symptom relief typically within 48 hours to 2 weeks; maximum effect by 4-6 weeks
- **Durability**: Sustained efficacy demonstrated over 4+ years in long-term studies
- **No effect on prostate volume**: Do not reduce prostate size or alter natural history of BPH
- **No reduction in AUR or surgery risk**: Unlike 5-alpha reductase inhibitors, alpha-blockers do not prevent disease progression

## Side Effects

### Cardiovascular
- **Orthostatic hypotension**: More common with non-selective agents (doxazosin, terazosin); less common with tamsulosin and silodosin
- **Dizziness**: 5-10% overall; dose-related; advise taking at bedtime initially
- **Syncope**: Rare (<1%); associated with first-dose effect in non-selective agents
- **Nasal congestion/rhinitis**: Due to alpha-1 blockade in nasal vasculature

### Ejaculatory
- **Retrograde or anejaculation**: Most common with silodosin (~28%), tamsulosin (~10%), less with alfuzosin (~1%)
- Mechanism: Blockade of alpha-1A receptors at the bladder neck impairs bladder neck closure during ejaculation
- Reversible upon discontinuation

### Ophthalmic
- **Intraoperative floppy iris syndrome (IFIS)**: Iris billowing, prolapse, and progressive pupil constriction during cataract surgery
- Most strongly associated with tamsulosin; reported with all alpha-blockers
- Discontinuation before surgery does NOT reliably prevent IFIS (iris smooth muscle atrophy may persist)
- Recommendation: Inform ophthalmologist of current or prior alpha-blocker use; ideally initiate alpha-blocker after cataract surgery if surgery is planned

### Other
- Asthenia and fatigue (2-5%)
- Headache (2-4%)

## Contraindications and Precautions

- **Absolute contraindications**: Hypersensitivity to the drug; hepatic impairment (alfuzosin contraindicated in severe hepatic insufficiency); silodosin contraindicated with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole) or severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min)
- **Relative contraindications**: Planned cataract surgery (discuss timing with ophthalmology); history of orthostatic hypotension; concurrent antihypertensives (additive hypotension risk)
- **Drug interactions**: CYP3A4 inhibitors increase silodosin and tamsulosin levels; PDE5 inhibitors may potentiate hypotensive effect; avoid concurrent use of multiple alpha-blocking agents

## Monitoring

- Symptom assessment (IPSS) at 4-6 weeks after initiation, then every 6-12 months
- Blood pressure monitoring, particularly after initiation or dose titration (non-selective agents)
- Assessment of ejaculatory function (particularly relevant in sexually active younger men)
- Annual DRE and PSA (alpha-blockers do not affect PSA levels, unlike 5-alpha reductase inhibitors)
- Inquire about planned eye surgery at each visit
