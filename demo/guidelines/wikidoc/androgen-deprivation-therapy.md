# Androgen Deprivation Therapy (ADT)

## Definition

Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) is the systemic suppression of testosterone to castrate levels, defined as serum testosterone <50 ng/dL (1.7 nmol/L), with the therapeutic goal ideally <20 ng/dL. ADT is a cornerstone of prostate cancer treatment, exploiting the androgen dependence of prostatic epithelial cells to induce tumor regression. Originally achieved by bilateral orchiectomy (surgical castration), modern ADT primarily uses pharmacologic agents including GnRH agonists, GnRH antagonists, and the oral GnRH antagonist relugolix.

## Mechanism of Action

### GnRH Agonists (Leuprolide, Goserelin, Triptorelin)
- Act on the anterior pituitary GnRH receptors
- Initial stimulation causes a transient surge in LH and testosterone lasting 1-3 weeks ("testosterone flare")
- Sustained exposure leads to GnRH receptor downregulation, suppression of LH release, and castrate testosterone levels by 2-4 weeks
- Flare prevention: co-administer first-generation antiandrogen (bicalutamide 50 mg) for 2-4 weeks starting 1 week before or concurrently with the first GnRH agonist injection
- Formulations: monthly, 3-monthly, 6-monthly, or yearly depot injections

### GnRH Antagonists (Degarelix, Relugolix)
- Competitive blockade of GnRH receptors on pituitary gonadotrophs
- Immediate suppression of LH, FSH, and testosterone without flare
- Castrate levels achieved within 1-3 days

**Degarelix**: Subcutaneous injection; loading dose 240 mg, then 80 mg monthly. Injection site reactions common (~40%). Advantage: no flare, rapid onset, possibly lower cardiovascular event risk (per retrospective analyses).

**Relugolix (Orgovyx)**: Oral GnRH antagonist; loading dose 360 mg day 1, then 120 mg daily. HERO trial demonstrated non-inferiority to leuprolide with superior testosterone suppression rate (96.7% vs 88.8%) and significantly lower cardiovascular events (2.9% vs 6.2%). Advantage: oral administration, rapid off-and-on capability, lower cardiovascular risk.

### Bilateral Orchiectomy
- Irreversible surgical castration; achieves castrate testosterone within 12 hours
- No flare, no compliance concerns, lower cost
- Psychological impact limits acceptance; considered for patients with spinal cord compression or requiring immediate castration

## Indications

- **Metastatic prostate cancer** (mCSPC and mCRPC): Backbone of all systemic regimens
- **Locally advanced prostate cancer**: Combined with EBRT (short-term 4-6 months for unfavorable intermediate; long-term 2-3 years for high/very high risk)
- **Biochemical recurrence**: Especially with PSA doubling time <10-12 months or adverse pathology
- **Neoadjuvant/adjuvant to radiation**: Improves overall survival in intermediate and high-risk disease
- **Palliation**: Bone pain, ureteral obstruction, hematuria from advanced disease
- **Contraindication to definitive local therapy**: Patients unfit for surgery or radiation

## Clinical Features (Side Effects)

ADT produces a predictable constellation of side effects due to systemic testosterone depletion:

**Vasomotor**: Hot flashes (55-80%), often distressing; managed with venlafaxine 37.5-75 mg, medroxyprogesterone, or cyproterone acetate

**Sexual**: Erectile dysfunction (>80%), decreased libido (>90%), penile and testicular atrophy, loss of ejaculatory volume

**Body composition**: Weight gain (average 2-4 kg in first year), increased visceral fat, decreased lean muscle mass (sarcopenia), fatigue

**Metabolic syndrome**: Insulin resistance, increased diabetes risk (44% higher), dyslipidemia (increased triglycerides, LDL; decreased HDL), increased waist circumference

**Cardiovascular**: Increased risk of coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, and sudden cardiac death (10-20% increased risk); greatest risk in men with pre-existing cardiovascular disease. Relugolix may have lower CV risk vs. GnRH agonists.

**Skeletal**: Accelerated bone loss (2-5% BMD decrease per year), increased fracture risk (up to 20% at 5 years), osteoporosis

**Cognitive and psychological**: Cognitive decline (memory, executive function), depression, mood changes, emotional lability, fatigue

**Hematologic**: Anemia (normocytic, Hb typically decreases by 1-2 g/dL)

**Other**: Gynecomastia and breast tenderness (especially with antiandrogen monotherapy), arthralgias

## Diagnosis (Monitoring Protocol)

### Baseline Assessment (Before Starting ADT)
- Serum testosterone, PSA, CBC, metabolic panel, HbA1c, fasting lipids
- DEXA bone density scan
- Cardiovascular risk assessment (Framingham score or equivalent)
- Assessment of baseline sexual function, cognitive function, mood
- Body composition and exercise capacity

### Ongoing Monitoring
- **Testosterone**: Every 3-6 months; confirm castrate level (<50 ng/dL, ideally <20 ng/dL); if not suppressed, switch agent or add/change therapy
- **PSA**: Every 3-6 months; rising PSA on ADT defines castration resistance (2 consecutive rises >=50% above nadir with absolute PSA >2.0 ng/mL)
- **DEXA scan**: At baseline, repeat every 1-2 years
- **Metabolic panel, HbA1c, fasting lipids**: Every 6-12 months
- **CBC**: Every 6-12 months (monitor for anemia)
- **Body weight and waist circumference**: Every visit
- **Screening for depression/cognitive changes**: Periodic assessment

## Treatment (Management of Side Effects)

### Bone Health
- **Calcium**: 1200 mg/day (diet + supplementation)
- **Vitamin D**: 2000 IU/day (target 25-OH vitamin D >30 ng/mL)
- **Denosumab 60 mg SC every 6 months**: If T-score <=-2.5 or high FRAX fracture risk (different from bone metastasis dose of 120 mg monthly)
- **Zoledronic acid 5 mg IV annually**: Alternative for osteoporosis prevention
- **Weight-bearing and resistance exercise**: Proven to maintain BMD and reduce fracture risk

### Cardiovascular and Metabolic Risk
- Lifestyle: structured exercise program (150 min/week moderate aerobic + resistance training), dietary counseling (Mediterranean diet)
- Manage modifiable risk factors aggressively: hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia
- Cardiology consultation for pre-existing cardiovascular disease
- Consider relugolix over GnRH agonists in men with significant cardiovascular risk (HERO trial)

### Intermittent vs. Continuous ADT
- Intermittent ADT (IAD): Cycles of ADT with treatment-free intervals triggered by PSA response (typically: treat until PSA <0.2-4 ng/mL for 6-9 months, then pause; restart when PSA rises to a predefined threshold)
- Non-inferior overall survival for metastatic disease in select patients (SWOG 9346: trend toward inferiority in M1 disease with minimal metastatic burden; NCIC PR7: non-inferior for biochemical recurrence)
- Benefits of IAD: improved quality of life during off-treatment intervals (sexual function recovery, fewer hot flashes, improved energy and mood), potential reduction in metabolic and cardiovascular complications
- Not recommended for high-volume metastatic disease

## Prognosis

- ADT achieves biochemical response (PSA decline) in >90% of hormone-sensitive prostate cancer
- Median duration of response: 18-24 months for metastatic disease before progression to castration resistance
- Modern combination strategies (ADT + novel antiandrogen or chemotherapy) have extended median overall survival in mCSPC to >50 months
- Quality of life impact is substantial; proactive management of side effects and involvement of multidisciplinary team (oncology, endocrinology, cardiology, physiotherapy) improves outcomes
- Patients on long-term ADT (>3 years) require ongoing survivorship care focused on bone health, cardiovascular risk, metabolic syndrome, and psychological well-being
