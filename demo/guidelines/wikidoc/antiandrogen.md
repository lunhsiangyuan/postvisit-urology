# Novel Antiandrogens (Second-Generation AR Pathway Inhibitors)

## Definition

Novel antiandrogens, also termed second-generation androgen receptor (AR) pathway inhibitors, are a class of agents that potently inhibit AR signaling through direct AR antagonism or upstream blockade of androgen biosynthesis. Unlike first-generation antiandrogens (bicalutamide, flutamide, nilutamide) which have partial agonist activity, these agents demonstrate no agonist activity and maintain efficacy in the setting of AR overexpression. They have fundamentally transformed the treatment of both metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mCSPC) and metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC).

## Mechanism of Action

### AR Inhibitors (Enzalutamide, Apalutamide, Darolutamide)
These agents target multiple steps of the AR signaling pathway:
- Competitive inhibition of androgen binding to the AR ligand-binding domain
- Inhibition of AR nuclear translocation
- Inhibition of AR binding to DNA and co-activator recruitment
- No partial agonist activity (unlike bicalutamide)

### CYP17A1 Inhibitor (Abiraterone)
- Irreversible inhibition of CYP17A1 (17-alpha-hydroxylase and 17,20-lyase)
- Blocks androgen biosynthesis in the testes, adrenal glands, and intratumoral steroidogenesis
- Reduces testosterone, DHEA, and androstenedione to undetectable levels
- Must be co-administered with prednisone/prednisolone (5-10 mg daily) to prevent mineralocorticoid excess from upstream CYP17 blockade

## Pharmacology

### Enzalutamide (Xtandi)
- **Dose**: 160 mg (four 40 mg capsules) once daily, with or without food
- **Half-life**: 5.8 days (active metabolite N-desmethyl-enzalutamide: 7.8 days)
- **Metabolism**: CYP2C8 (primary) and CYP3A4; strong CYP3A4 inducer and moderate CYP2C8 inducer
- **Renal adjustment**: No adjustment needed
- **Key trials**:
  - **mCRPC**: AFFIRM (post-docetaxel, OS benefit 18.4 vs 13.6 months) and PREVAIL (pre-docetaxel, 72% reduction in radiographic progression risk)
  - **mCSPC**: ENZAMET (OS benefit), ARCHES (rPFS benefit)
  - **nmCRPC**: PROSPER (MFS 36.6 vs 14.7 months)

### Apalutamide (Erleada)
- **Dose**: 240 mg (four 60 mg tablets) once daily, with or without food
- **Half-life**: 3 days (active metabolite N-desmethyl-apalutamide: 4 days)
- **Metabolism**: CYP2C8 and CYP3A4; strong CYP3A4 and CYP2C19 inducer
- **Key trials**:
  - **mCSPC**: TITAN (OS and rPFS benefit; 35% reduction in death risk)
  - **nmCRPC**: SPARTAN (MFS 40.5 vs 16.2 months; first FDA approval in nmCRPC)

### Darolutamide (Nubeqa)
- **Dose**: 600 mg (two 300 mg tablets) twice daily with food
- **Half-life**: 20 hours (active metabolite keto-darolutamide similar potency)
- **Metabolism**: CYP3A4, UGT1A9, UGT1A1; does NOT significantly induce or inhibit CYP enzymes
- **Unique properties**: Minimal blood-brain barrier penetration (lower CNS side effects); distinct chemical structure reduces cross-reactivity
- **Key trials**:
  - **mCSPC**: ARASENS (OS benefit with ADT + darolutamide + docetaxel triplet; 32.5% reduction in death)
  - **nmCRPC**: ARAMIS (MFS 40.4 vs 18.4 months)

### Abiraterone Acetate (Zytiga)
- **Dose**: 1000 mg (two 500 mg or four 250 mg tablets) once daily on empty stomach + prednisone 5 mg daily (mCSPC) or prednisone 5-10 mg daily (mCRPC)
- **Alternative**: Abiraterone acetate fine particle formulation (Yonsa) 500 mg with food
- **Half-life**: 12 hours (active metabolite abiraterone: 15 hours)
- **Metabolism**: CYP3A4, SULT2A1; inhibits CYP2D6, CYP2C8
- **Key trials**:
  - **mCSPC**: LATITUDE (OS benefit for high-risk mCSPC; 38% reduction in death), STAMPEDE (OS benefit)
  - **mCRPC**: COU-AA-301 (post-docetaxel, OS 15.8 vs 11.2 months), COU-AA-302 (pre-docetaxel, OS 34.7 vs 30.3 months)

## Clinical Features (Side Effects)

### Enzalutamide
- Fatigue (36%), hypertension (13%), falls (11%), dizziness (10%)
- Seizure risk (~0.5%): lower threshold in patients with predisposing conditions; contraindicated in seizure history
- Cognitive impairment (memory, attention) due to CNS penetration
- Hot flashes (20%)

### Apalutamide
- Skin rash (24%, including grade 3-4 in 5%; typically maculopapular, onset weeks 1-4, may require dose interruption)
- Hypothyroidism (8%): monitor TSH; may require levothyroxine
- Fractures (12%): ensure bone protective agents
- Fatigue (21%), arthralgia (16%), hot flashes (14%)
- Falls (16%)

### Darolutamide
- Fatigue (16%), nausea (9%), pain in extremities (6%)
- Neutropenia: when combined with docetaxel (ARASENS regimen), monitor CBC
- **Favorable CNS safety profile**: Lowest rates of falls, seizures, and cognitive impairment among AR inhibitors
- Rash less common than apalutamide

### Abiraterone
- **Mineralocorticoid excess** (due to upstream CYP17 blockade and cortisol suppression):
  - Hypertension (22%), hypokalemia (17%), fluid retention/edema (25%)
  - Mitigated by concomitant prednisone; monitor electrolytes closely
- Hepatotoxicity: ALT/AST elevation (10-15%); monitor LFTs monthly for first 3 months, then periodically
- Fatigue (39%), joint pain (19%), hot flashes (22%)
- Cardiac: atrial fibrillation, cardiac failure (rare but reported)

## Drug Interactions

- **Enzalutamide**: Strong CYP3A4 inducer — reduces efficacy of many drugs (warfarin, opioids, certain antibiotics, antifungals, immunosuppressants); CYP2C8 substrate — avoid strong CYP2C8 inhibitors (gemfibrozil)
- **Apalutamide**: Strong CYP3A4 inducer — similar interaction profile to enzalutamide; CYP2C8 substrate
- **Darolutamide**: Minimal CYP induction — significantly fewer drug interactions; preferred in patients on polypharmacy (especially relevant in elderly men with comorbidities)
- **Abiraterone**: CYP2D6 inhibitor — increases levels of metoprolol, codeine (may reduce conversion to morphine), dextromethorphan; CYP2C8 inhibitor — avoid co-administration with pioglitazone, repaglinide

## Treatment (Clinical Application and Sequencing)

### mCSPC
- All four agents are combined with ADT backbone
- Choice depends on: disease volume/risk, comorbidities, drug interactions, side effect profile, patient preference, cost
- Triplet therapy: ADT + darolutamide + docetaxel (ARASENS) or ADT + abiraterone + docetaxel (PEACE-1) for high-volume disease

### nmCRPC (Non-Metastatic Castration-Resistant)
- Enzalutamide, apalutamide, or darolutamide, all with continued ADT
- Indicated when PSA doubling time <=10 months with no detectable metastases on conventional imaging

### mCRPC
- Choice of agent depends on prior therapy in mCSPC setting
- Cross-resistance: Prior exposure to one novel AR-targeted agent reduces response to another (AR amplification, splice variants such as AR-V7, glucocorticoid receptor activation)
- Sequencing principle: switch mechanism of action (e.g., AR inhibitor -> taxane chemotherapy or PARP inhibitor for HRR-mutated disease)

### Biomarker-Guided Therapy
- **HRR mutations (BRCA2, BRCA1, ATM, CDK12)**: PARP inhibitor (olaparib, rucaparib) indicated after progression on novel antiandrogen
- **AR-V7 splice variant**: Detected in circulating tumor cells; predicts resistance to AR-targeted therapy; favors taxane-based chemotherapy
- **MSI-H/dMMR**: Pembrolizumab (tumor-agnostic indication)

## Prognosis

- Novel antiandrogens have transformed mCSPC outcomes: median OS extended to >50 months in combination regimens
- nmCRPC: Metastasis-free survival extended by approximately 2 years across PROSPER, SPARTAN, and ARAMIS trials
- mCRPC: Overall survival improved by 4-5 months over placebo in registration trials; sequential therapy further extends survival
- Triplet therapy (ADT + novel antiandrogen + docetaxel) provides the greatest survival benefit in high-volume mCSPC
- Cross-resistance between AR-targeted agents limits sequential AR-targeted strategies; mechanism-switching is essential for optimal outcomes
- Genomic profiling (somatic and germline HRR testing) is now standard of care in mCRPC and should be offered to all patients with metastatic disease to identify actionable mutations
