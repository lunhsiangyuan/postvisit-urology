# Prostate Cancer

## Definition

Prostate cancer is the most common solid malignancy in men and the second leading cause of cancer death in males worldwide. The vast majority (>95%) are acinar adenocarcinomas arising from the peripheral zone of the prostate gland. Clinically significant prostate cancer is distinguished from indolent disease by its potential for local invasion, lymph node involvement, and distant metastasis, particularly to bone.

## Pathophysiology

Prostate carcinogenesis is driven by androgen receptor (AR) signaling. Testosterone is converted to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) by 5-alpha reductase within prostatic epithelial cells, activating the AR to promote cell proliferation and survival.

The disease typically progresses through a stepwise model:
- **Proliferative inflammatory atrophy (PIA)**: Chronic inflammation-associated lesion
- **High-grade prostatic intraepithelial neoplasia (HGPIN)**: Premalignant lesion with cytologic atypia but intact basal cell layer
- **Invasive adenocarcinoma**: Loss of basal cell layer, stromal invasion
- **Metastatic disease**: Lymphatic and hematogenous spread (predilection for axial skeleton via Batson's vertebral venous plexus)

Key molecular alterations include TMPRSS2-ERG gene fusion (~50% of cases), PTEN loss, TP53 mutations, and homologous recombination repair (HRR) gene mutations (BRCA2, ATM, CDK12) found in ~25% of metastatic castration-resistant disease.

## Classification

### Gleason Score and ISUP Grade Groups
- **Grade Group 1**: Gleason 3+3=6 (indolent, favorable)
- **Grade Group 2**: Gleason 3+4=7 (favorable intermediate)
- **Grade Group 3**: Gleason 4+3=7 (unfavorable intermediate)
- **Grade Group 4**: Gleason 4+4=8 (high grade)
- **Grade Group 5**: Gleason 4+5, 5+4, or 5+5=9-10 (highest grade)

### TNM Staging (AJCC 8th Edition)
- **T1**: Clinically inapparent (T1a: incidental <=5%, T1b: incidental >5%, T1c: needle biopsy for elevated PSA)
- **T2**: Organ-confined (T2a: <=half of one lobe, T2b: >half of one lobe, T2c: both lobes)
- **T3**: Extracapsular extension (T3a: extraprostatic, T3b: seminal vesicle invasion)
- **T4**: Fixed or invading adjacent structures (bladder, rectum, pelvic wall)
- **N0/N1**: No / regional lymph node metastasis
- **M1a/M1b/M1c**: Non-regional lymph nodes / bone / visceral metastasis

### NCCN Risk Stratification
- **Very low**: T1c, GG1, PSA <10, <3 positive cores, <=50% cancer per core, PSA density <0.15
- **Low**: T1-T2a, GG1, PSA <10
- **Favorable intermediate**: T2b-T2c or GG2 (with <10% Gleason pattern 4) or PSA 10-20, only 1 intermediate risk factor
- **Unfavorable intermediate**: T2b-T2c or GG2-3 or PSA 10-20, with >=2 intermediate risk factors or GG3
- **High**: T3a or GG4-5 or PSA >20
- **Very high**: T3b-T4 or primary Gleason pattern 5 or >4 cores with GG4-5
- **Metastatic**: Any T, N1 or M1

## Diagnosis

- **PSA (prostate-specific antigen)**: Normal <4.0 ng/mL; gray zone 4-10 ng/mL; free-to-total PSA ratio <10% suspicious; PSA density >0.15 ng/mL/cc suspicious; PSA velocity >0.75 ng/mL/year concerning
- **Digital rectal examination**: Asymmetry, nodules, induration
- **Multiparametric MRI (mpMRI)**: PI-RADS scoring (1-5); PI-RADS >=3 warrants targeted biopsy
- **Prostate biopsy**: Systematic 12-core + MRI-targeted (MRI-TRUS fusion or cognitive fusion); transperineal approach preferred (lower sepsis risk)
- **Genomic biomarkers**: Decipher (genomic classifier for metastatic risk), Oncotype DX GPS (17-gene assay), Prolaris (cell-cycle progression score), ConfirmMDx (epigenetic assay for repeat biopsy decisions)
- **PSMA PET/CT**: Gallium-68 or F-18 PSMA PET for staging in unfavorable intermediate, high-risk, or biochemical recurrence settings

## Treatment

### Active Surveillance (Very Low, Low, Select Favorable Intermediate)
- PSA every 6 months, DRE every 12 months
- mpMRI every 12-24 months
- Confirmatory biopsy at 12-24 months, then serial biopsies per protocol
- Reclassification triggers: upgrade to GG >=2, increase in volume or number of positive cores

### Localized Disease (Intermediate Risk)
- **Favorable intermediate**: Active surveillance (if GG2 with <10% pattern 4), radical prostatectomy (RALRP), EBRT with or without short-term ADT (4-6 months)
- **Unfavorable intermediate**: RALRP with extended pelvic lymph node dissection (ePLND), EBRT + short-term ADT (4-6 months), brachytherapy boost options

### Localized Disease (High and Very High Risk)
- RALRP with extended PLND (if surgically feasible, no fixation)
- EBRT + long-term ADT (2-3 years) per DART 01/05 and EORTC 22961 data
- Multimodal approach: surgery followed by adjuvant or early salvage radiation for adverse pathology (pT3, positive margins, detectable PSA)

### Metastatic Hormone-Sensitive Prostate Cancer (mCSPC)
- ADT backbone (GnRH agonist or antagonist) combined with:
  - Novel antiandrogen: enzalutamide (ENZAMET), apalutamide (TITAN), darolutamide (ARASENS)
  - Docetaxel (CHAARTED, STAMPEDE): primarily high-volume disease
  - Abiraterone + prednisone (LATITUDE, STAMPEDE)
  - Triplet therapy: ADT + darolutamide + docetaxel (ARASENS) or ADT + abiraterone + docetaxel for high-volume disease (PEACE-1)

### Metastatic Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer (mCRPC)
- Sequencing: novel antiandrogen (if not used in mCSPC) -> docetaxel or cabazitaxel -> PARP inhibitor (olaparib, rucaparib for HRR-mutated) -> Lu-177 PSMA-617 (VISION trial, post-taxane and post-novel antiandrogen) -> radium-223 (bone-predominant, ALSYMPCA)
- Pembrolizumab for MSI-H/dMMR tumors

### Key Surgical Concepts
- Nerve-sparing (bilateral or unilateral) based on oncologic safety and erectile function goals
- Extended PLND: obturator, external iliac, internal iliac, presacral nodes (when estimated LN risk >5%)
- Focal therapy (HIFU, cryotherapy): select low-to-intermediate risk, unilateral disease, within clinical trial or registry settings

### Radiation Therapy Considerations
- **EBRT**: Intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) or volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT); dose escalation to 78-80 Gy improves biochemical control
- **Brachytherapy**: Low-dose-rate (LDR) permanent seed implant for low/favorable intermediate risk; high-dose-rate (HDR) boost combined with EBRT for intermediate/high risk
- **Stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT)**: Emerging option with 5-fraction hypofractionation (35-36.25 Gy); non-inferior to conventional fractionation in phase III data
- **Post-prostatectomy radiation**: Adjuvant (within 6 months for adverse pathology) vs. early salvage (at PSA >0.1-0.2 ng/mL); ARTISTIC meta-analysis supports early salvage over adjuvant in most cases

## Prognosis

- **Localized disease**: 5-year relative survival approximately 99%
- **Regional (lymph node positive)**: 5-year relative survival approximately 99%
- **Distant metastatic**: 5-year relative survival approximately 34%
- ISUP Grade Group, PSA kinetics, genomic classifiers (Decipher), and metastatic burden (high vs. low volume) are key prognostic determinants
- Presence of HRR gene mutations (BRCA2) associated with more aggressive disease but actionable with PARP inhibitors
- Early intensification of systemic therapy in mCSPC has demonstrated overall survival benefit across all recent phase III trials
- Biochemical recurrence after radical prostatectomy: defined as PSA >=0.2 ng/mL on two consecutive measurements; 5-year BCR-free survival ranges from >95% (very low risk) to ~60% (high risk)
- Metastatic volume classification (CHAARTED criteria): high-volume (>=4 bone metastases with >=1 outside axial skeleton, or visceral metastases) carries worse prognosis but benefits most from treatment intensification
