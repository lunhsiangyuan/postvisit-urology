# Enzalutamide

## Drug Name and Class

**Generic Name:** Enzalutamide
**Brand Name:** Xtandi
**Drug Class:** Androgen receptor inhibitor (novel antiandrogen)
**Routes:** Oral (capsules, 40 mg; tablets, 40 mg and 80 mg)

## Indications

Enzalutamide is indicated for the treatment of:

- **Metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer (mCSPC)**
- **Non-metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (nmCRPC)** — in patients at high risk of metastasis (PSA doubling time <= 10 months)
- **Metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC)** — including post-docetaxel and chemotherapy-naive settings

Used in combination with a GnRH analog or in patients who have undergone bilateral orchiectomy.

## Dosage and Administration

| Indication | Dose | Administration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| All approved indications | 160 mg (four 40 mg capsules) once daily | With or without food | Swallow whole with water |
| With strong CYP2C8 inhibitor | 80 mg once daily | — | Reduce dose if gemfibrozil co-administered |
| Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) | 80 mg once daily | — | Reduced hepatic metabolism |

Continue GnRH analog concurrently unless prior bilateral orchiectomy. If grade >= 3 toxicity or intolerable side effect occurs, withhold for one week or until symptoms improve to grade <= 2, then resume at same or reduced dose (120 mg or 80 mg).

## Warnings and Precautions

- **Seizures:** Incidence approximately 0.5%. CONTRAINDICATED in patients with history of seizures, underlying brain injury, stroke, brain metastases, or conditions that may lower seizure threshold. Avoid concurrent medications that lower seizure threshold.
- **Posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES):** Rare. Presents with seizure, headache, visual disturbance, confusion, altered mental status. Discontinue if PRES suspected.
- **Ischemic heart disease:** Increased incidence of ischemic cardiac events (3.7% vs 1.3% placebo). Monitor cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Falls and fractures:** Increased risk (11% falls, 10% fractures). Evaluate fall risk and bone health. Consider bone-protective agents.
- **Embryo-fetal toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during and for 3 months after treatment.

## Adverse Reactions

**Most common (>= 10%):**
Fatigue/asthenic conditions (36%), musculoskeletal pain (15%), hot flush (13%), hypertension (12%), diarrhea (10%), fall (11%).

**Common (1-10%):**
Headache (9%), dizziness (7%), hematuria (7%), upper respiratory infection (6%), weight decreased, cognitive disorder, gynecomastia, fractures.

**Less common (<1%):**
Seizures (0.5%), PRES, neutropenia, hypersensitivity reactions (angioedema, tongue edema).

**Postmarketing:** Toxic epidermal necrolysis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome reported.

## Drug Interactions

| Interacting Drug | Effect |
|---|---|
| Strong CYP2C8 inhibitors (gemfibrozil) | Increased enzalutamide AUC by 2.2x — reduce dose to 80 mg daily |
| CYP3A4 substrates (midazolam, triazolam) | Enzalutamide is a STRONG CYP3A4 INDUCER — reduces substrate exposure significantly |
| CYP2C9 substrates (warfarin) | Reduced warfarin efficacy — monitor INR frequently, adjust dose |
| CYP2C19 substrates (omeprazole) | Reduced efficacy — consider alternative agents |
| P-gp substrates (digoxin, dabigatran) | May reduce substrate exposure — monitor therapeutic levels |
| Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (itraconazole) | May increase enzalutamide exposure — no dose adjustment, but use caution |

**Clinical note:** Enzalutamide is one of the strongest CYP3A4 inducers in oncology. Review ALL concomitant medications for potential interactions.

## Use in Specific Populations

**Pregnancy:** Not indicated for use in females. Enzalutamide can cause fetal harm based on mechanism of action and animal data.

**Lactation:** Not applicable (male patients only).

**Pediatric Patients:** Safety and efficacy not established in pediatric patients.

**Geriatric Patients:** No overall differences in safety/efficacy in patients >= 65 years. No dose adjustment. Median age in clinical trials was 69 years.

**Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild-to-severe renal impairment. Not studied in patients on dialysis.

**Hepatic Impairment:** Mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B): no dose adjustment. Severe (Child-Pugh C): reduce to 80 mg once daily.

**Male Fertility:** May impair fertility based on animal findings. Reversibility has not been established.
