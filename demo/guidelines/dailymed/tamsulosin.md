# Tamsulosin Hydrochloride

## Drug Name and Class

**Generic Name:** Tamsulosin Hydrochloride
**Brand Name:** Flomax
**Drug Class:** Selective alpha-1A adrenergic receptor blocker
**Routes:** Oral (capsules, 0.4 mg)

## Indications

Tamsulosin is indicated for:

- **Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)** — treatment of signs and symptoms, including incomplete emptying, frequency, intermittency, urgency, weak stream, straining, and nocturia. Tamsulosin relaxes smooth muscle in the prostate and bladder neck to improve urine flow and reduce obstructive symptoms.

## Dosage and Administration

| Population | Starting Dose | Maximum Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults | 0.4 mg once daily | 0.8 mg once daily | Take 30 min after the same meal each day |
| Dose escalation | 0.4 mg daily x 2-4 weeks | 0.8 mg daily | Increase only if inadequate response at 0.4 mg |
| Missed dose > several days | Restart at 0.4 mg | 0.8 mg daily | Re-titrate even if previously on 0.8 mg |

Capsules should be swallowed whole — do not crush, chew, or open. Food effect: must be taken in the fed state (30 minutes after a meal) to ensure consistent absorption.

## Warnings and Precautions

- **Orthostatic hypotension:** Postural hypotension (dizziness, vertigo) may occur, especially with the first dose or dose escalation. Caution when rising from sitting or lying position.
- **Syncope:** Has been reported, particularly within the first few doses. Advise patients to sit or lie down at onset of dizziness.
- **Priapism:** Rare but serious. Patients must seek immediate medical attention for erections lasting > 4 hours.
- **Intraoperative floppy iris syndrome (IFIS):** CRITICAL — observed during cataract and glaucoma surgery in patients taking or previously exposed to alpha-1 blockers. Patients MUST inform their ophthalmologist before any eye surgery, even if tamsulosin has been discontinued.
- **Sulfonamide allergy:** Tamsulosin contains a sulfonamide group. Caution advised in patients with history of sulfonamide allergy, though clinical cross-reactivity is rare.

## Adverse Reactions

**Most common (>= 5%):**
Abnormal ejaculation (8-18%, dose-dependent — higher at 0.8 mg), dizziness (6%), rhinitis (4%).

**Common (1-5%):**
Headache, asthenia, back pain, diarrhea, pharyngitis, somnolence, decreased libido, insomnia.

**Less common (<1%):**
Priapism, rash, pruritus, syncope, palpitations, blurred vision, nausea.

**Postmarketing:** Skin desquamation (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, rare), intraoperative floppy iris syndrome, constipation, vomiting.

## Drug Interactions

| Interacting Drug | Effect |
|---|---|
| Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, itraconazole) | Significantly increased tamsulosin exposure — avoid concomitant use |
| Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (paroxetine, fluoxetine, terbinafine) | Increased tamsulosin levels (2-3 fold) — use with caution |
| Other alpha-1 blockers (doxazosin, terazosin, alfuzosin) | Additive hypotension — avoid concurrent use |
| PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) | Additive hypotension — use with caution, separate dosing |
| Warfarin | No clinically significant interaction — no dose adjustment needed |
| Cimetidine | Decreased clearance of tamsulosin — minor effect, monitor |

## Use in Specific Populations

**Pregnancy:** Not applicable. Tamsulosin is not indicated for use in women.

**Lactation:** Not applicable.

**Pediatric Patients:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children. Tamsulosin is not indicated for pediatric use.

**Geriatric Patients:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed in patients >= 65 years vs. younger adults. No dose adjustment required.

**Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment (CrCl < 10 mL/min) — use with caution. Not removed by hemodialysis.

**Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild impairment. Moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment has not been studied — use is not recommended.
