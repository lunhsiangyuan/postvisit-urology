# Leuprolide Acetate

## Drug Name and Class

**Generic Name:** Leuprolide Acetate
**Brand Name:** Lupron Depot, Eligard
**Drug Class:** GnRH agonist (gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist)
**Routes:** Intramuscular injection (Lupron Depot), Subcutaneous injection (Eligard)

## Indications

Leuprolide acetate is indicated for:

- **Palliative treatment of advanced prostate cancer** — as an alternative to orchiectomy for testosterone suppression
- **Endometriosis** — management of endometriosis and associated pain (Lupron Depot 3.75 mg monthly)
- **Uterine leiomyomata (fibroids)** — preoperative hematologic improvement with concurrent iron therapy
- **Central precocious puberty** — children with onset before age 8 (females) or 9 (males)

## Dosage and Administration

| Formulation | Dose | Route | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lupron Depot | 7.5 mg | IM | Every month |
| Lupron Depot | 22.5 mg | IM | Every 3 months |
| Lupron Depot | 30 mg | IM | Every 4 months |
| Lupron Depot | 45 mg | IM | Every 6 months |
| Eligard | 7.5 mg | SC | Every month |
| Eligard | 22.5 mg | SC | Every 3 months |
| Eligard | 30 mg | SC | Every 4 months |
| Eligard | 45 mg | SC | Every 6 months |

Reconstitute depot formulations immediately before injection. Rotate injection sites. Verify testosterone suppression with periodic serum testosterone levels (target: < 50 ng/dL).

## Warnings and Precautions

- **Testosterone flare:** Initial surge of testosterone occurs in the first 1-2 weeks of therapy. Can cause transient worsening of bone pain, urinary obstruction, spinal cord compression, and renal impairment. Consider concurrent anti-androgen (e.g., bicalutamide) for the first 2-4 weeks to mitigate flare.
- **Hyperglycemia and diabetes:** Increased risk of new-onset or worsening diabetes mellitus. Monitor blood glucose and HbA1c periodically.
- **Cardiovascular disease:** Increased risk of myocardial infarction, sudden cardiac death, and stroke. Evaluate cardiovascular risk before initiating and monitor during treatment.
- **QT prolongation:** Androgen deprivation therapy may prolong QTc interval. Consider risks/benefits in patients with history of QT prolongation or concurrent QT-prolonging medications.
- **Convulsions:** Reported in patients with and without predisposing conditions. Manage risk factors.
- **Embryo-fetal toxicity:** Contraindicated in pregnancy. Major risk of spontaneous abortion.

## Adverse Reactions

**Most common (>= 10%):**
Hot flashes/flushes (58%), injection site reactions (12-35% depending on formulation), fatigue (10%), general pain (12%).

**Common (1-10%):**
Musculoskeletal pain (8%), edema (7%), nausea (5%), testicular atrophy, headache, insomnia, dizziness, decreased libido, gynecomastia, depression.

**Less common (<1%):**
Anaphylaxis, pulmonary embolism, pituitary apoplexy (with pre-existing pituitary adenoma), spinal fracture/paralysis (testosterone flare), interstitial lung disease.

**Laboratory changes:** Elevated LDH, alkaline phosphatase, BUN, creatinine, lipids, liver transaminases. Decreased RBC count, hemoglobin.

## Drug Interactions

| Interacting Drug | Effect |
|---|---|
| QT-prolonging drugs (sotalol, amiodarone, fluoroquinolones) | Additive QT prolongation — monitor ECG |
| Drugs affecting pituitary gonadotropins | May interfere with therapeutic effect — avoid concurrent use |
| Hypoglycemic agents (insulin, metformin) | May require dose adjustment — monitor glucose closely |

Leuprolide is not metabolized by cytochrome P450 enzymes and has no significant CYP-mediated drug interactions.

## Use in Specific Populations

**Pregnancy:** Contraindicated (Pregnancy Category X). Can cause spontaneous abortion and fetal harm. Exclude pregnancy before initiating therapy in females.

**Lactation:** Unknown if excreted in breast milk. Contraindicated during breastfeeding due to potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants.

**Pediatric Patients:** Used for central precocious puberty. Lupron Depot-Ped formulations available. Monitor height, bone age, and gonadotropin levels.

**Geriatric Patients:** No overall differences in safety/efficacy. Majority of prostate cancer patients are elderly. Monitor for cardiovascular and metabolic complications.

**Renal Impairment:** No specific dose adjustment required. Pharmacokinetics not formally studied in renal impairment.

**Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dose adjustment required. Pharmacokinetics not formally studied in hepatic impairment.

**Monitoring:** Serum testosterone (confirm < 50 ng/dL by month 2), PSA levels, bone density (prolonged therapy), glucose, lipid panel, and cardiovascular risk assessment.
