# Atorvastatin Calcium

## Drug Name and Class

**Generic Name:** Atorvastatin Calcium
**Brand Name:** Lipitor
**Drug Class:** HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin)
**Routes:** Oral (tablets: 10 mg, 20 mg, 40 mg, 80 mg)

## Indications

Atorvastatin is indicated for:

- **Hyperlipidemia:** As an adjunct to diet to reduce elevated total cholesterol, LDL-C, apolipoprotein B, and triglycerides in patients with primary hypercholesterolemia and mixed dyslipidemia
- **Primary prevention of cardiovascular disease:** To reduce risk of MI, stroke, revascularization, and angina in adults without clinically evident CHD but with multiple risk factors (age, smoking, hypertension, low HDL-C, family history)
- **Secondary prevention:** To reduce risk of nonfatal MI, fatal and nonfatal stroke, revascularization, hospitalization for CHF, and angina in patients with clinically evident CHD
- **Pediatric patients (10-17 years):** Heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia as adjunct to diet after failed dietary trial

## Dosage and Administration

| Population | Starting Dose | Maximum Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults (general) | 10-20 mg once daily | 80 mg once daily | Administer at any time of day, with or without food |
| Established CHD / high risk | 40 mg once daily | 80 mg once daily | Higher starting dose for aggressive LDL lowering |
| With cyclosporine | 10 mg once daily | 10 mg once daily | Do not exceed 10 mg |
| With tipranavir + ritonavir | Avoid | — | Significant interaction |
| With clarithromycin, itraconazole | 20 mg once daily | 40 mg once daily | Use lowest effective dose |
| Pediatric (10-17 years) | 10 mg once daily | 20 mg once daily | For heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia |

Dose adjustments based on LDL-C reduction goals: 10 mg reduces LDL-C ~39%, 80 mg reduces ~60%. Check lipid panel 2-4 weeks after initiation or dose change.

## Warnings and Precautions

- **Hepatotoxicity:** Perform liver function tests (ALT, AST) before initiating and as clinically indicated. Discontinue if persistent elevations > 3x ULN. Contraindicated in active liver disease or unexplained persistent transaminase elevations.
- **Rhabdomyolysis/Myopathy:** Risk increases with higher doses, advanced age, renal impairment, hypothyroidism, and concurrent CYP3A4 inhibitors, fibrates, or niacin. Discontinue immediately if markedly elevated CPK (> 10x ULN) or myopathy diagnosed.
- **New-onset diabetes mellitus:** Class effect of statins. Modest increase in HbA1c and fasting glucose. Cardiovascular benefit outweighs diabetes risk in most patients.
- **Cognitive effects:** Reversible cognitive impairment (memory loss, confusion) reported postmarketing. Generally resolves upon discontinuation.
- **Interstitial lung disease:** Rare. Consider if dyspnea, dry cough, or general malaise develop during therapy.
- **Immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM):** Rare autoimmune condition with proximal muscle weakness and elevated CPK, not resolving upon statin discontinuation. Anti-HMG-CoA reductase antibodies positive.

## Adverse Reactions

**Most common (>= 5%):**
Nasopharyngitis (8.3%), arthralgia (6.9%), diarrhea (6.8%), pain in extremity (6.0%), urinary tract infection (5.7%).

**Common (2-5%):**
Myalgia (5.4%), headache, insomnia, nausea, dyspepsia, musculoskeletal pain, pharyngolaryngeal pain.

**Less common (<2%):**
Rhabdomyolysis, hepatitis, angioedema, pancreatitis, alopecia, erythema multiforme, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy.

**Postmarketing:** Rhabdomyolysis with acute renal failure, immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy, fatal and nonfatal hepatic failure, depression, gynecomastia.

## Drug Interactions

| Interacting Drug | Effect |
|---|---|
| Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (clarithromycin, itraconazole, ritonavir, HIV protease inhibitors) | Significantly increased atorvastatin exposure — use lowest dose, avoid if possible |
| Cyclosporine | Markedly increased atorvastatin levels — do not exceed 10 mg daily |
| Gemfibrozil | Increased myopathy/rhabdomyolysis risk — avoid combination |
| Niacin (>= 1 g/day) | Increased myopathy risk — weigh risks/benefits carefully |
| Colchicine | Increased myopathy risk — use with caution |
| Digoxin | Increased digoxin levels (~20%) — monitor digoxin levels |
| Oral contraceptives (norethindrone, ethinyl estradiol) | Increased hormone levels (~30%) — consider when selecting OC dose |
| Warfarin | Slight decrease in prothrombin time early; monitor INR at initiation |
| Rifampin | Reduced atorvastatin exposure (simultaneous administration) — administer simultaneously if needed, not staggered |
| Grapefruit juice (large quantities) | Increased atorvastatin levels — avoid excessive consumption |

## Use in Specific Populations

**Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. Statins may cause fetal harm. Discontinue immediately upon pregnancy recognition. Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception.

**Lactation:** Contraindicated. Atorvastatin is present in rat milk; human data unknown. Due to potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, breastfeeding is not recommended.

**Pediatric Patients:** Approved for ages 10-17 years (heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, females must be at least 1 year post-menarche). Max dose 20 mg/day. Long-term safety not established.

**Geriatric Patients:** No dose adjustment. Patients >= 65 years may be at increased risk for myopathy. Plasma levels approximately 40% higher in elderly.

**Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required. Renal disease does not affect atorvastatin plasma concentrations.

**Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver disease or unexplained persistent transaminase elevations. Exposure increased in patients with chronic alcoholic liver disease (Child-Pugh B).

**Perioperative Considerations:** Some surgeons hold statins 24-48 hours before major surgery due to rhabdomyolysis concern. Evidence is mixed; discuss risk-benefit with surgical team.
