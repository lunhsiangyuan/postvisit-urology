# Denosumab

## Drug Name and Class

**Generic Name:** Denosumab
**Brand Name:** Prolia (osteoporosis), Xgeva (bone metastases)
**Drug Class:** RANK ligand (RANKL) inhibitor — fully human IgG2 monoclonal antibody
**Routes:** Subcutaneous injection

## Indications

Denosumab is indicated for:

- **Prolia (60 mg):**
  - Treatment of osteoporosis in men at high risk for fracture, including men receiving androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) for nonmetastatic prostate cancer
  - Treatment of postmenopausal osteoporosis at high risk for fracture
  - Treatment of glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis

- **Xgeva (120 mg):**
  - Prevention of skeletal-related events (SREs) in patients with bone metastases from solid tumors
  - Treatment of giant cell tumor of bone
  - Treatment of hypercalcemia of malignancy refractory to bisphosphonate therapy

## Dosage and Administration

| Product | Dose | Route | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prolia | 60 mg | SC (upper arm, thigh, or abdomen) | Every 6 months | Supplement with calcium 1000 mg + vitamin D 400 IU daily |
| Xgeva | 120 mg | SC | Every 4 weeks | Supplement with calcium and vitamin D; additional 120 mg on days 8 and 15 of first month for hypercalcemia |

Administer by healthcare professional. Do not use Prolia and Xgeva concurrently (same active ingredient, different doses/indications).

## Warnings and Precautions

- **Hypocalcemia:** Correct pre-existing hypocalcemia BEFORE initiating. Supplement all patients with calcium and vitamin D. Monitor calcium levels, especially within first 2 weeks. Higher risk in patients with CrCl < 30 mL/min.
- **Osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ):** Incidence 1-2% with Xgeva, 0.04% with Prolia. Risk factors: invasive dental procedures, poor oral hygiene, concurrent therapy (corticosteroids, angiogenesis inhibitors). Perform dental examination before starting; avoid invasive dental procedures during treatment.
- **Atypical femoral fractures:** Reported with long-term use. Evaluate patients with thigh or groin pain for incomplete femoral fractures.
- **Serious infections:** Increased incidence of serious skin infections (cellulitis), endocarditis, and urinary tract infections. Immunosuppressive effect of RANKL inhibition. Advise prompt medical attention for signs of infection.
- **Dermatologic reactions:** Dermatitis, eczema, and rash reported. Discontinue if severe reactions occur.
- **Rebound bone loss on discontinuation:** Multiple vertebral fractures reported within 7-10 months of stopping denosumab (especially Prolia). Transition to an alternative antiresorptive (bisphosphonate) upon discontinuation.

## Adverse Reactions

**Most common (>= 5%):**
Musculoskeletal pain (12%), extremity pain (10%), back pain (8%), fatigue (6%), hypophosphatemia (Xgeva: 32%).

**Common (1-5%):**
Nausea, diarrhea, hypocalcemia (Xgeva: 9.6%, Prolia: 2%), headache, anemia, dyspnea, decreased appetite.

**Less common (<1%):**
Osteonecrosis of the jaw, atypical femoral fracture, anaphylaxis, pancreatitis, new primary malignancy (not confirmed causal).

**Postmarketing:** Severe symptomatic hypocalcemia (including fatal cases), musculoskeletal pain (sometimes severe), lichenoid drug eruptions.

## Drug Interactions

| Interacting Drug | Effect |
|---|---|
| Other immunosuppressants (corticosteroids, chemotherapy) | Additive immunosuppression — increased infection risk |
| Other antiresorptive agents (bisphosphonates) | Concurrent use not recommended (ONJ risk); sequential use acceptable for transition |
| Drugs causing hypocalcemia | Additive risk — monitor calcium levels closely |

Denosumab is not metabolized by cytochrome P450 enzymes. No significant pharmacokinetic drug interactions identified.

## Use in Specific Populations

**Pregnancy:** Contraindicated (Pregnancy Category X for Prolia). Based on animal data, may cause fetal harm including increased fetal loss, stillbirths, and skeletal abnormalities. Verify negative pregnancy test before each dose in women of reproductive potential.

**Lactation:** Unknown if excreted in human milk. Denosumab was detected in monkey milk. Weigh risks vs. benefits.

**Pediatric Patients:** Xgeva approved for skeletally mature adolescents with giant cell tumor of bone. Not approved in pediatric osteoporosis (may impair bone growth).

**Geriatric Patients:** No overall differences in safety/efficacy in patients >= 65 years. No dose adjustment required.

**Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment. However, patients with CrCl < 30 mL/min or on dialysis are at significantly greater risk of hypocalcemia. Monitor calcium frequently.

**Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied. No dose adjustment recommended as hepatic metabolism is not expected for monoclonal antibodies.

**Monitoring:** Serum calcium (baseline and periodic), vitamin D levels, serum phosphorus, magnesium, dental health assessment, bone mineral density (Prolia).
